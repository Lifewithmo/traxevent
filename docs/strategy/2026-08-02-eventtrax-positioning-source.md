## The core reframe

The insight is not *what* these businesses sell — it's *how they operate*: they book a date, travel to a customer, and deliver a job. That shared operating pattern is the product.

> Positioning line proposed in the thread:
> **"EventTrax — the operating system for businesses that book jobs, travel to customers, and deliver unforgettable events."**
>
> "The moment someone books a date on a calendar, EventTrax takes over until that job is complete."

Explicitly **not** positioned as: event management, CRM, or POS. Positioned as an **Operating System for Appointment & Event Businesses**.

## The 8 "common-denominator" questions

Every one of these businesses asks:

1. Who is the customer?
2. What are we providing?
3. When is the event?
4. Where is the event?
5. Who's working it?
6. What equipment or inventory do we need?
7. How do we get paid?
8. How do we follow up?

## The signature product idea — "Choose your industry" → install modules

Replace the generic signup question with an industry picker (Coffee Cart · Caterer · Food Truck · Florist · Photographer · DJ · Rentals · Bartender · Beauty · Other), then install industry-specific modules. Same platform, different modules.

Named per-vertical module sets:

- **Florist:** floral recipes · delivery schedule · wedding timeline · inventory of flowers · setup checklist
- **Food truck:** daily menu · commissary checklist · inventory · festival calendar · POS integration
- **Photographer:** shot list · timeline · gallery delivery · second shooter · equipment checklist

## The caterer end-to-end workflow (cited as near-perfect fit)

Inquiry → Menu & headcount → Proposal → Contract → Deposit → Staffing → Purchasing & prep → Equipment loading → Event execution → Final invoice → Reporting

## Market sizing (directional, IBISWorld-based)

| Market | US businesses | EventTrax fit | Serviceable | Share of initial market |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Photographers | ~250,600 | 35–45% | ~88k–113k | ~50–55% |
| Food trucks / mobile food | ~88,400 | 60–75% | ~53k–66k | ~30–35% |
| Florists | ~37,300 | 20–35% | ~7.5k–13k | ~5–7% |
| Caterers | ~13,700 | 80–90% | ~11k–12.3k | ~6–7% |

Headline ~390,000 US businesses across the four; serviceable ~160k–205k. At 1% penetration and ~$69/mo → ~$1.3–1.7M ARR before add-ons.

## Recommended launch order

1. Coffee carts / mobile beverage vendors — clean niche, strong event workflow
2. Caterers / mobile bartenders — highest operational pain, best willingness to pay
3. Event florists — complex jobs, meaningful contract values, few purpose-built tools
4. Food trucks — large but needs stronger POS + inventory
5. Photographers — largest market but most established competition

## Competitive landscape (fragmented)

- **General platforms:** Square, QuickBooks, Google Workspace, Wix/Squarespace, Calendly, Jotform, Stripe, spreadsheets, Gmail
- **General client-management:** HoneyBook, Dubsado, 17hats, Planning Pod, Maroo
- **Photography:** Studio Ninja, Sprout Studio, Pixifi, Iris Works, Bloom, Pixieset, ShootQ, Táve, Zenfolio
- **Catering:** Caterease, Tripleseat, Total Party Planner, CaterZen, Better Cater, Flex Catering, FoodStorm, CaterTrax, Perfect Venue
- **Florist:** Details Flowers, Curate, Floranext, BloomNation, FloristWare, Lobiloo, Lovingly
- **Food trucks / coffee carts:** Square, Toast, Clover, Hotplate, StreetFoodFinder, FoodTruck.pub — fragmented, no dominant lead-to-event platform (→ cleaner entry point)

The real competitor is the **patchwork**: Instagram + Jotform + Gmail + Google Calendar + Square + QuickBooks + Notes + spreadsheets. EventTrax must beat that on convenience.

**Strongest initial opening:** businesses that operate around booked events, travel to the customer, need event-specific menus/staff/equipment/inventory, find HoneyBook too generic and catering platforms too expensive — i.e. coffee carts, mobile bartenders, small caterers, specialty food vendors, event florists, before photographers.
