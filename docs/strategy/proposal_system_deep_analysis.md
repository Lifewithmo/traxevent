# Deep analysis: what makes a great proposal system

A great proposal system should not merely produce a nice-looking PDF.

It should turn an opportunity into a **clear buying decision**, capture exactly what the customer selected, secure approval and payment, and hand the accepted scope into the next stage of the business without anyone retyping it.

The complete flow should be:

> **Opportunity → Scope → Price → Options → Proposal → Customer decision → Signature → Deposit → Job or project**

That is the standard your CRM should target.

---

# 1. The proposal-software market

The market currently falls into three groups.

### Document-first systems

- PandaDoc
- Proposify
- Better Proposals

These prioritize templates, document creation, signatures, content libraries and sales-team control.

### Web-presentation systems

- Qwilr

These treat the proposal more like an interactive website than a traditional document.

### Business-workflow systems

- HoneyBook
- Jobber
- Housecall Pro

These may produce less sophisticated documents, but they connect proposals more effectively to payments, client portals, scheduling, jobs and operational workflows.

Your product should sit between these categories:

> **As fast as Jobber, as visually polished as Qwilr, as structured as PandaDoc, and as connected to the customer workflow as HoneyBook.**

# 2. PandaDoc

## Position

PandaDoc is the broadest dedicated document platform in this comparison. It handles proposals, quotes, contracts, content libraries, approvals, e-signatures, payments and document analytics. It is more of a document operating system than a lightweight proposal feature. ([pandadoc.com](https://www.pandadoc.com/product-overview/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Five best things

1. **Strong reusable templates and content blocks.** Approved sections, pricing tables and standard language can be reused across documents.
2. **Complete document lifecycle.** Creation, review, internal approval, sending, tracking, signing and payment exist in one platform.
3. **Flexible pricing tables.** Products, quantities, discounts, taxes and line items can be structured rather than manually typed.
4. **Large integration ecosystem.** It works well as the document layer on top of a CRM.
5. **Good usability relative to its depth.** Reviewers frequently praise its setup speed, templates and ability to send documents quickly. ([g2.com](https://www.g2.com/products/pandadoc/reviews?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Five worst things

1. **It can become bloated for a small business.**
2. **Advanced proposal features push customers into higher plans.**
3. **Formatting behavior can be inconsistent**, especially when attempting highly customized layouts.
4. **Billing and renewal complaints appear more often outside traditional software-review platforms.**
5. **It is document-centric, not job-centric.** An accepted proposal often still needs another system to schedule and deliver the work. ([g2.com](https://www.g2.com/products/pandadoc/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## What to copy

- Content library
- Structured pricing tables
- Internal approvals
- Version tracking
- E-signature and payment in one flow

## What not to copy

- A general-purpose document editor with dozens of controls
- Complicated pricing tiers
- A proposal that becomes disconnected from the actual work after acceptance

# 3. Proposify

## Position

Proposify is more narrowly focused on controlled sales proposals. It emphasizes branded templates, interactive pricing, content consistency, approvals and engagement tracking. It is particularly suited to sales teams that want managers to control what representatives can change. ([proposify.com](https://www.proposify.com/proposal-software?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

1. **Strong brand consistency.** Teams can standardize layouts, sections and pricing presentation.
2. **Interactive quoting.** Customers can adjust quantities and options and immediately see the impact on price.
3. **Good internal control.** Approved language and approval workflows help prevent salespeople from changing sensitive terms.
4. **Proposal analytics.** Teams can see views, engagement and proposal progress.
5. **Reusable templates save meaningful preparation time.** This is one of the most frequently praised review themes. ([g2.com](https://www.g2.com/products/proposify/reviews?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Five worst things

1. **The editor can feel restrictive or awkward.**
2. **Fine-tuning layouts may take too much work.**
3. **Users report occasional bugs and formatting problems.**
4. **It is less suited to simple, mobile-first field quoting.**
5. **The operational handoff remains shallow.** It closes the proposal but does not inherently run the resulting job. ([qwilr.com](https://qwilr.com/blog/proposify-review/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## What to copy

- Locked content blocks
- Approval rules
- Interactive quote calculations
- Customer-adjustable quantities
- Engagement tracking

## What not to copy

- Desktop-oriented proposal production
- Excessive template administration
- Making visual layout more important than scope accuracy

# Cross-analysis

## What nearly all the systems share

Most modern proposal products now provide some combination of:

- Templates
- Branded presentation
- Pricing tables
- E-signature
- View notifications
- Online acceptance
- Payment integration
- Reusable service or content libraries

That is now table stakes. A new system will not win merely because it can make a proposal, collect a signature and notify the seller when the document is opened. ([g2.com](https://www.g2.com/categories/proposal?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## Where the real separation occurs

The meaningful differences are:

1. **How flexible is pricing?**
2. **How attractive is the buyer experience?**
3. **How fast can a normal employee create the proposal?**
4. **Does accepted scope become operational data?**
5. **Can managers control margins, discounts and legal language?**
6. **Can the customer configure the purchase without creating confusion?**
7. **Can scope changes be managed after acceptance?**

# What your proposal system should be

## Product promise

> **Build it once, let the customer choose, get it approved and paid, and turn it into work—without re-entering anything.**

The proposal should not be an attached file inside the CRM. It should be a structured part of the opportunity.

---

# The proposal data model

A proposal should contain structured data rather than being stored as one large block of formatted content.

## Core record

- Proposal name
- Customer
- Opportunity
- Proposal owner
- Status
- Expiration date
- Version
- Currency
- Tax settings
- Total value
- Selected value
- Signature status
- Payment status

## Content

- Introduction
- Customer problem
- Recommended solution
- Scope of work
- Timeline
- Images and videos
- Testimonials
- Terms
- Exclusions
- Assumptions
- Warranty information

## Pricing

- Service or product
- Description
- Quantity
- Unit
- Internal cost
- Markup
- Customer price
- Taxability
- Required or optional
- One-time or recurring
- Package membership
- Discount
- Allowance

## Customer decisions

- Selected package
- Selected options
- Quantities chosen
- Requested changes
- Signature
- Deposit
- Financing choice

This means the accepted proposal becomes an exact, immutable snapshot of what was sold.

# Proposal, estimate and contract

These should be related but not treated as identical.

### Estimate

The mathematical price calculation.

### Proposal

The customer-facing explanation of the solution, scope, choices and investment.

### Contract

The binding terms and obligations governing the accepted work.

Your system should let the business choose between:

- **Quick quote:** Pricing and approval
- **Full proposal:** Story, scope, options, terms and approval
- **Proposal plus contract:** Everything signed in one experience

Do not force a landscaper quoting a $450 cleanup to create the same document as a contractor proposing a $150,000 installation.

# The seven parts of the proposal machine

## 1. Create

The user should be able to start from:

- An industry template
- A previous proposal
- A saved package
- The current opportunity
- A blank proposal
- AI-generated first draft

CRM data should automatically populate:

- Customer name
- Company
- Address
- Project name
- Salesperson
- Opportunity information
- Discovery notes

The user should not copy and paste customer information.

## 2. Define scope

Scope should be built from reusable blocks.

Examples:

- Site preparation
- Irrigation installation
- Photography coverage
- Catering service
- Monthly maintenance
- Project management
- Warranty

Each scope block can include:

- Description
- Deliverables
- Included work
- Exclusions
- Photos
- Required customer decisions
- Related pricing items

The system should connect scope and price. Removing a scope section should remove or flag its related price.

## 3. Configure pricing

This is the most important technical component.

The pricing engine should support:

- Fixed price
- Unit price
- Hourly
- Quantity-based
- Recurring monthly or annual
- One-time setup plus recurring
- Minimum charges
- Discounts
- Taxes
- Deposits
- Milestone payments
- Allowances
- Optional items
- Bundles
- Good-better-best packages

### Example

**Good — $12,500**

- Basic landscape installation
- Standard plant package
- One-year workmanship warranty

**Better — $16,900**

- Everything in Good
- Premium plant package
- Landscape lighting
- Two-year warranty

**Best — $22,400**

- Everything in Better
- Outdoor seating area
- Smart irrigation controls
- Seasonal maintenance package

The customer chooses one package and can add optional items.

## 4. Present

The live proposal should:

- Work beautifully on mobile
- Match the company’s brand
- Load through a secure link
- Support photos and video
- Have simple section navigation
- Clearly display what is included
- Show the selected total continuously
- Allow PDF download
- Print correctly

Do not give customers a design canvas. Give businesses several polished layouts that cannot easily be ruined.

## 5. Decide and collaborate

The customer should be able to:

- Select a package
- Add optional items
- Adjust permitted quantities
- Ask a question on a section
- Request a change
- Accept or decline
- Select a payment schedule
- Choose financing where available

The business should see:

- First opened
- Last opened
- Number of views
- Sections viewed
- Options considered
- Comments
- Other viewers
- Time since last activity

The purpose is not surveillance. It is to tell the salesperson when and how to follow up.

## 6. Sign and pay

The acceptance experience should include:

- One or multiple signers
- Initials where necessary
- Signature order
- Acceptance checkbox
- Audit history
- Deposit requirement
- Credit card or ACH
- Payment schedule
- Optional automatic recurring payment authorization
- Receipt and confirmation

Acceptance, signature and payment should be separate states.

A proposal could be:

- Accepted but unsigned
- Signed but deposit pending
- Signed and paid
- Declined
- Expired

Do not overload one status field with every possible condition.

## 7. Convert to work

Upon acceptance, the system should automatically create whichever records the business uses:

- Job
- Project
- Event
- Booking
- Work order
- Contract
- Invoice
- Deposit invoice
- Onboarding checklist

The resulting job should inherit:

- Accepted scope
- Selected package
- Optional items
- Quantities
- Price
- Customer notes
- Attachments
- Timeline
- Payment schedule
- Terms
- Salesperson

This is where your product can beat dedicated proposal software.

# Proposal statuses

Use one controlled proposal status:

**Draft → Internal Review → Ready → Sent → Viewed → Changes Requested → Accepted / Declined / Expired**

Then track these separately:

### Signature status

- Not required
- Awaiting signature
- Partially signed
- Fully signed

### Payment status

- Not required
- Deposit pending
- Deposit paid
- Payment plan active
- Paid in full

### Work status

- Not created
- Created
- Scheduled
- In progress
- Complete

This avoids the tag problem we discussed earlier. Tags can describe the proposal, but they should not replace controlled transactional states.

# Essential controls

## Versioning

Every significant change after sending should create a new version.

The system should retain:

- What changed
- Who changed it
- When it changed
- Which version the customer accepted

Once accepted, the accepted version must be locked.

## Internal pricing privacy

Customers see:

- Description
- Quantity
- Price
- Total

Employees with permission can see:

- Cost
- Markup
- Gross profit
- Margin percentage
- Minimum permitted price

## Approval rules

Examples:

- Discount greater than 10% requires manager approval.
- Margin below 30% requires approval.
- Contract over $50,000 requires owner approval.
- Modified legal language requires approval.
- Financing option requires a minimum contract amount.

This adds control without making every proposal complicated.

---

# Automations

Keep the same sentence-style model as your CRM:

> **When this happens → do this.**

Examples:

- When a proposal is sent → schedule follow-up in three days.
- When the customer first views it → notify the salesperson.
- When it has been viewed three times → mark it as engaged.
- When no activity occurs for five days → create a follow-up task.
- When a change is requested → move the opportunity to Waiting on Us.
- When accepted → create the job and deposit invoice.
- When signed but unpaid for 24 hours → send a deposit reminder.
- When declined → request a lost reason.

# AI features that would actually help

## Discovery-to-proposal

AI can turn notes, emails or a voice memo into:

- Customer need
- Recommended scope
- Deliverables
- Assumptions
- Exclusions
- Suggested follow-up questions

## Scope quality check

AI should flag:

- Vague deliverables
- Missing quantities
- Conflicting dates
- Missing exclusions
- Services mentioned in the narrative but absent from pricing
- Pricing items without corresponding scope
- Undefined customer responsibilities

## Proposal summary

Generate a short customer-facing summary:

> Based on our site visit, we recommend replacing the front irrigation zone, installing a new planting bed and adding landscape lighting along the walkway.

## Follow-up drafting

Use actual engagement:

> Sarah reviewed the lighting and payment sections twice but has not selected a package. Draft a useful follow-up focused on those items.

AI should assist with clarity. It should not silently invent pricing, legal terms or scope.

# The real differentiation

Most proposal tools make one of two compromises:

1. They create beautiful documents that do not become operational work.
2. They create operational quotes that do not effectively sell the value.

Your product can bridge that gap.

## The positioning

> **A proposal that sells the job and becomes the job.**

Or:

> **Create it, let the customer choose, get approved and paid, then start the work—without entering anything twice.**

## The product principle

Every accepted customer selection must become structured business data.

That means:

- No manually interpreting the signed PDF
- No re-entering line items
- No guessing which optional services were selected
- No losing proposal exclusions
- No mismatch between sales and operations
- No salesperson promising something the field team cannot see

That is the proposal machine I would build inside your CRM.
